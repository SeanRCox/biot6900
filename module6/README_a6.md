In this module, I built a polygenic risk score (PRS) in a synthetic toy cohort, then followed the same workflow with a published Alzheimer’s disease score and 1000 Genomes genotype data. The notebooks demonstrate how effect alleles are harmonized to ALT-allele dosages, how variant weights are combined into a PRS, and how score distributions and performance can vary across ancestry groups. In the graded Part 3 notebook, I completed the workflow by comparing discrimination, calibration, and the added value of the PRS over age, and interpreting the results and their limitations.

To run the graded assignment, open `BIOT6900_Module6_Part3_Graded_Starter.ipynb` and run all cells from a restarted kernel. The four CSV files must be in the neighboring `data/` folder. The supplied genotype panel contains the 22 variants in Alzheimer’s PGS000334, so it supports that score; a different score would require a genotype panel containing its variants. The genotype data and published score are real, and the ancestry labels come from 1000 Genomes, but age and phenotype in `meta.csv` are simulated. Therefore, AUC and calibration are illustrative results from the simulation, not estimates of real-world disease prediction. The data sources, phenotype-generation details, expected results, and checksums are documented in `data/README.md`.
